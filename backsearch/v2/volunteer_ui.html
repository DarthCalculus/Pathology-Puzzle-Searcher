2What is happening

Each worker owns one job (a subtree named by its seed path) for a window. Inside the window it keeps a local stack of sub-trees; at the end it briefly probes what is left, deepest first (absorbing), and reports the whole tree once. Grey cells in the board are not decided yet.

3Levels

Depth = number of moves the level needs. The codes are Pathology level codes (rows separated by /): copy one to play it. A board holds still while the pointer is on it, so its code can be copied or selected.

5Campaign

"Roots covered" counts the top-level jobs whose whole subtree is finished. An exit is exact when it is covered and every unresolved candidate (a state whose shortcut check hit a capacity limit) deeper than its best has been checked. The total amount of work is unknown until it is done, so no percentage of the search is shown anywhere.

6How it works

A job
One node of the search tree and everything below it, named by its seed path (a list of backward moves such as U2,R3,L1). Any worker given the same seed explores exactly the same subtree, so nothing depends on who runs it or when.
Windows and splitting
A worker keeps a job for a window of 30 min (the server shortens it near the end of a campaign; each window uses the length current when it starts). A job that outlives its window prints the exact list of subtrees it has not finished; those become new jobs and the parent is marked split. The pieces are disjoint and together they are precisely the unexplored part, so nothing is lost or repeated. Trivial pieces are finished on this machine and never reach the server.
Pausing
Pause freezes the worker processes (SIGSTOP). They keep their memory, and while this client stays online their jobs stay reserved for up to 12 h after each lease. A paused laptop that also sleeps or goes offline for longer than that loses them; the work is simply done by someone else.
Stopping
Stop asks each worker to hand back its remaining work. That takes seconds: the worker prints its remaining subtrees, the client reports them, and only then exits. Closing this tab also stops the client.
When the campaign is complete
The client stops asking for work, delivers its last reports, shows the result here and in the terminal, and exits by itself (with --keep-going it waits for the next campaign instead).
Changing the worker count
Extra workers finish their current job and then retire; new workers start with the next lease (within 10 s).
Leases
A job is reserved for the client that took it for 1.0 h, renewed by every heartbeat. If that client vanishes (crash, power loss), the job simply returns to the pool afterwards and someone else picks it up. The client keeps at most two jobs per worker, so nothing waits here that another volunteer could run.
"Done" fractions
An exit is proven when every root job is covered: finished, or split with all children covered. The panel shows covered roots out of all roots plus CPU hours spent. Roots differ enormously in size, so this is not a percentage of the total work, and no such percentage exists before the end.
