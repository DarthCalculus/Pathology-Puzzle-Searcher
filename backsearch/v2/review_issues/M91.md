## Verifier (CONFIRMED, medium, refuted=False)
The claim holds.

Where the traces come from:
- test_chaos.sh:41 sets BS_TRACE_VALID=$T/tv/$1 in the client's environment.
- volunteer.py:743 starts workers with subprocess.Popen(argv, ...) and no env=, so every worker inherits it.
- backsearch.c:4873 opens `<name>.<pid>` per process.
- Line 79 loads glob `tv/*`, which is every run by every client, accepted or not.

Runs that are never accepted but still land in the union:
1. Orphaned workers of SIGKILLed A. They are started with start_new_session=True, and `pkill -9 -f "volunteer.py --name A"` does not match the backsearch argv, so they survive. Because split_after is 1 s, many chaos jobs finish inside their window. An orphan that exhausts before its next STATUS write exits normally and flushes a complete trace, yet no report ever reaches the server.
2. Absorption probes that split. volunteer.py:669-673 reports them as open nodes, and their partial coverage is dropped by design.
3. dup_fraction re-runs, and reports refused by first-report-wins or stealing.

How this masks a bug: take a regression in lease expiry or re-issue, for example the sweeper or the release-grace path marking a lapsed job done or losing its re-issue. The audit then shows it covered, the orphan's complete trace supplies its levels, and 'lost 0' passes. That is exactly the bug class the SIGKILL scenario exists to catch.

The fixed sleeps are unchecked. Lines 49-52 (`sleep 4; kill -9 "$A"`, `sleep 4; kill -INT "$B"`) are followed by no check that A held a lease, or that B's log shows a SIGINT-caused split tree report. Line 58's `split > 0` is met anyway by 1 s windows.

The union still catches an outright lost split child that no run ever explored. So the check is weakened rather than useless. Kept at medium because it is the main exactness gate for the server's coverage tree before the next campaign.

*fix notes*: Build the job-side union only from accepted work. One option is to have the client log `pid job_id seed outcome` per run and keep only traces whose (job, seed) the server holds as done or split. The other is to read $T/data/jobs.db and, for each trace line, require that its path lies under a done node or in the explored part of a split node (under the node's seed but not under any of its children's seeds). Assert that A held at least one lease when it was killed, for example by grepping client_A.log for 'window' lines before the kill, and that client_B.log shows a split tree report after the SIGINT. Replace the fixed sleeps with polls on those conditions.
