# M32: Fingerprint duplicates mostly check trivial jobs; heavy ones split and become incomparable, and steal/reclaim bypass the different-client rule

verdict CONFIRMED, severity low, category algorithmic
locations: jobs.js:813 (uniform coin flip; only 'done' first nodes re-issued); openOfExit (dup must not return to the original's client); stealJob / heartbeat reclaim

## Summary
Re-issue is a uniform coin flip per finished job, and only 'done' first nodes qualify, so most checks cover sub-second jobs, and split jobs are never checked. A dup runs with whatever window the lease grants (120 s during the ramp and the tail), so dups of long jobs split and end up 'incomparable'. stealJob and heartbeat reclaim ignore openOfExit's rule that a dup must not go back to the original's client, and dup jobs were stolen and ping-ponged after the tree was complete.

## Source report F85 (server-scheduling)
**Fingerprint duplicates barely check heavy jobs, and steal/reclaim bypass the different-client rule** at /Users/george/PathologyRecords/server/lib/jobs.js:813

*summary*: Only 'done' first nodes are re-issued, and a dup runs with whatever window the lease grants (120 s during the ramp and the tail). Dups of long jobs therefore split and end up 'incomparable', and split jobs are never checked. stealJob and heartbeat reclaim ignore openOfExit's rule that a dup must not go back to the original's client.

*failure_scenario*: The heavy jobs, which are the ones most likely to evict (1,675 reports had evict_* > 0), are re-issued but leased at the tail with a 120-s window, so they split and cannot be compared. The fingerprint sample ends up covering mostly sub-2-minute jobs (matched dups' originals average 15 s). A client can also steal or reclaim a dup of its own job, so it checks itself.

*evidence*: 813: `} else if (status === 'done' && c.dup_fraction > 0 && Math.random() < c.dup_fraction) {`. 805: comparison only when `status === 'done' && orig.status === 'done'`. 268: stealJob and 532: reclaim have no dup_of condition, unlike openOfExit 274. Snapshot: 24 dups split:incomparable; 18 of the 46 dups whose original ran more than 120 s split; split jobs = 253.6 of 597 CPU-h and are never re-issued; 2 dups were finished by the original's own token and 26 by the same name.

*suggested_fix*: Lease dups with split_after_s >= 2 x the original's elapsed_s. Apply the dup exclusion (by name, not only by token) in stealJob and reclaim.

## Verifier (CONFIRMED, low, refuted=False)
The premises hold in the code and in the snapshot. Two of the proposed fixes are worth doing. The "sample proportional to elapsed" part of F175 is wrong: at the same CPU budget it gains nothing.

CODE
- jobs.js:813 `} else if (status === 'done' && c.dup_fraction > 0 && Math.random() < c.dup_fraction) {` is a uniform coin flip. It fires only for the reported job itself when that job exhausted. Split jobs, and done children that arrive inside a split's tree report, are never re-issued.
- jobs.js:805 compares only when both jobs are done: `if (orig && status === 'done' && orig.status === 'done' && orig.src_hash === hashIn)`. Anything else is 'incomparable'.
- jobs.js:582 `const splitAfter = open < 3 * workersActive ? c.ramp_split_after_s : c.split_after_s;` is one value for the whole batch (returned at :632). No job gets its own window, so a dup gets 120 s whenever the pool is thin.
- The client already honours a per-job value. volunteer.py:498 reads `sa = float(j["split_after_s"]) if j.get("split_after_s") is not None else resp_sa`, and :569 uses it as the deadline. The fix is therefore server-only.
- The different-client rule is token-only and exists only in openOfExit (jobs.js:274 `... (SELECT o.client_token FROM jobs o WHERE o.id = j.dup_of) IS NOT ?`).
  - stealJob (:268, used at :624) has no dup condition.
  - Reclaim (:532) has none either. It only hands back a job the client already held, so it adds a violation only after a steal.
  - DESIGN.md:22-23 promises "re-issued to a different client". DESIGN.md:367-368 says "Re-issued duplicate jobs are stealable like any other job". The two lines contradict each other.
- Exactness is not at risk. A dup's subtree is never inserted (:786). Dups are excluded from treeOfExit, rootExists and exitSummary. bestOfExit may return a dup row, but it holds the same level as its original and loses the tie on id.

SNAPSHOT (campaign 1, dup_fraction 0.02)
- 1,033 re-issues: 1,009 done:match, 24 split:incomparable, 0 mismatches.
- Originals: 727 took under 1 s, 89 took 60 s or more, 46 took more than 120 s. Of those 46, 18 split (39%). Of the 26 dups whose original evicted, 11 were lost this way; only 15 of the 1,164 evicting done jobs were ever matched.
- Why heavy dups land in the 120 s window:
  - A dup inherits its original's depth: about 13 on average for originals over 120 s.
  - Leasing is deepest-first (openOfExit ORDER BY depth DESC), so these dups wait behind the children of splits. The dups that split waited 6,631 s on average from creation to finish; those that finished done waited 2,666 s.
  - Ramp windows dominated from hour 7 on: 120-s splits per hour were 198/273/363/473 against 46/32/27/3 at 1800 s.
- Coverage:
  - Verified: 4.30 CPU-h and 294M states, which is 0.72% of CPU and 0.81% of states.
  - Split jobs hold 253.6 of 597 CPU-h and 13.3B of 36.4B states, and are never re-issued. That is inherent: a split's (states, valid, best) depends on when the timer fired, so it cannot serve as a fingerprint.
- Same-machine checks: 26 of 1,033 dups were finished under the original's own name. 24 of those were Panacea's other tokens, which openOfExit itself allowed because it compares tokens. Only 2 had the same token (steal or reclaim). The campaign had just 5 names on 7 tokens.
- Churn after the tree finished:
  - The tree finished at +40,514 s; the last dup finished at +53,911 s.
  - After tree completion there were 57 dup reports (1.31 CPU-h), 50 of them repeats. F175's figure of 103 reports and 2.97 CPU-h mixes in 55 reports on non-dup jobs.
  - Dup 19080 (original 16557 ran 269 s) was first run at +53,911 with a 120 s window, split to incomparable, and was reported 35 times.
  - The same churn hits non-dup job 167664 (41 reports). The root cause is tail stealing of running jobs, which is a separate issue; excluding dups from stealing removes only the dup share.

FEASIBILITY (my own estimates)
1. Give each dup its own window: split_after_s = max(current, 3x the original's elapsed_s), capped at about 2 h, returned as j.split_after_s. openOfExit can also select the original's elapsed_s. Leases are extended by heartbeats, so a long window is safe. On campaign #1 this would have saved all 24 incomparable checks: heavy matches 28 -> about 46, evicting 15 -> about 26, and verified CPU 4.3 -> about 6.7 h (0.72% -> about 1.1% of campaign CPU). It would also have avoided the 0.81 CPU-h wasted on dup runs cut off at 120 s. It is a few lines plus one gate test. Worth doing before the next campaign.
2. Exclude by name, in openOfExit and in the steal candidate query. This recovers about 2.5% of checks, the ones that could only test a machine against itself. Cheap; worth doing.
3. Exclude dups from stealing, or at least from the steal of running jobs. This removes the post-completion dup churn, about 1.3 CPU-h in campaign #1. Cheap; worth doing.
4. Proportional sampling is refuted.
   - At the same CPU budget as uniform 2% (6.64 CPU-h over 50,096 done leased jobs):

     | Scheme | Checks | Heavy (over 120 s) | Evicting |
     |---|---|---|---|
     | uniform 2% | 1,002 | 38 | 23 |
     | min(1, k*e), calibrated | 42 | 30 | 22 |
     | sqrt(e) | 92 | 36 | 24 |

   - Uniform sampling already spends p of the CPU in expectation, 72% of it on heavy jobs. The roughly 960 trivial checks cost almost nothing (under 0.3 CPU-h).
   - "2% of states instead of 0.8%" cannot happen at the same budget: verified states track CPU spent. With done-only eligibility, 2% of done jobs caps at about 1.2% of all states (22.4B of 36.4B states are in done leased jobs). The gap from 0.8% is the incomparable loss plus heavy-tail variance.
   - Verifying more needs a larger dup_fraction, not a different weighting.

Overall: low severity (these checks catch nondeterminism; they do not prove the result). The per-job dup window and the name-based rule are cheap, carry no exactness risk, and should go in before the next campaign.

(3) In leasedOthers (:266) add `AND j.dup_of IS NULL`, or at least skip running dups in the steal loop (:620-629).
(4) Reclaim (:532) can stay as it is once (2) and (3) are in.

(6) Gate test: re-issue a dup of a job with elapsed_s = 500 while the pool is thin (ramp active). Assert that the lease returns split_after_s >= 1500 for the dup and 120 for the others. Assert that a second token with the same name cannot lease or steal the dup.
