# M27: Tail work stealing thrashes: freshly leased or stolen jobs look 'queued' and are stolen again first, and victims are SIGKILLed

verdict CONFIRMED, severity high, category algorithmic
locations: jobs.js:616 (stealJob ranking: newest lease first, running_json from the holder's last heartbeat, tookRunning cap); heartbeat `drop`; volunteer.py:508 (no client-side dedupe amplifies it)

## Summary
When the pool is empty, every lease request from a client with idle workers steals at least one job. The holder's running_json is refreshed only by its heartbeat, up to 30 s old, so a job granted or stolen since then ranks as 'queued'. With newest-lease-first ordering it is the first zero-cost victim, and taking it never sets tookRunning. Each remaining job bounces between clients about once per lease request (every 10 s per client). Each thief starts it immediately, the previous holder SIGKILLs its copy on the next `drop`, and a client can be handed back a job it is still running and start a second copy. The result is many concurrent copies, lost progress and a later finish.

## Source report F76 (server-scheduling)
**Work stealing thrashes at the campaign tail: freshly stolen jobs look 'queued', victims are SIGKILLed, and clients steal back jobs they are still running** at /Users/george/PathologyRecords/server/lib/jobs.js:616

*summary*: When the pool is empty, every lease from a client with idle workers steals at least one job. The holder's running_json is refreshed only by its heartbeat, so a job granted or stolen since then ranks as 'queued', and with newest-lease-first ordering it is the first thing stolen again. The previous holder SIGKILLs its copy on its next heartbeat (drop). A client can also re-acquire a job it is still running, and nothing dedupes by job id, so it starts another concurrent copy.

*failure_scenario*: The pool empties with R heavy jobs in flight. Client T steals J (leased_at=now). Until T's next heartbeat (up to 30 s), J is missing from T's running_json, so it has rank 0 and, as the newest lease, sorts first. Idle client U's next lease (every 10 s) steals J again, and T's next heartbeat returns J in drop, which SIGKILLs T's copy. If J later comes back to T, T starts a second copy while the first keeps running (T holds J again, so no drop). In campaign #1 the copies piled up: job 167664 was reported 41 times by 4 clients between 09:17:09 and 09:20:04, 14 times by Deathranger999 (15 workers). Dup job 19080 was reported 35 times. With the current client's reliable drop-kill and 10 clients, a copy of a 120-s window has to be held by the same client at about 4 consecutive heartbeats to survive, so tail jobs rarely finish (close to a livelock).

*evidence*: jobs.js 616-617: `const rank = j => [runningOf.get(j.client_token).has(j.id) ? 1 : 0, -heldBy.get(j.client_token), -j.leased_at];` 623: `if (isRunning && tookRunning) break;` (one running job per request, but every request steals). 267: leasedOthers `WHERE ... j.client_token != ?` excludes only jobs the requester holds right now. 500: running_json is updated only in heartbeat. volunteer.py 1202-1210: drop -> `self._signal(s, signal.SIGKILL)`. volunteer.py 508: `self.queue.extend(good)` with no id dedupe. Snapshot, 08:45-09:25: 'dup' reports 17.5 CPU-h vs 12.0 CPU-h accepted. Hour 09: 725 of 1,312 reports were 'dup'. Campaign total: 1,411 dup reports = 31.6 CPU-h; killed runs are not even recorded.

*suggested_fix*: (1) Store running_at with running_json. Treat any job with leased_at >= the holder's running_at as possibly running, and never steal it as 'queued'. (2) Do not steal a job listed as running by a holder whose last_seen is within 2*heartbeat_s; steal running jobs only from stale holders. (3) Never grant (lease, steal or reclaim) a job id that is in the requester's own last running/holding list. (4) Limit re-steals of the same job, e.g. no second steal within one window. (5) In the client, dedupe incoming jobs against slots and queue.

*expected_gain*: Removes most of the tail's duplicate CPU (88% of reported CPU in the final 20 minutes) and the livelock risk once drop-kill works everywhere.

## Source report F94 (server-ingest-routes-tools)
**Work-stealing cascade: freshly stolen jobs look 'queued', get re-stolen on every lease request and run many times at once** at /Users/george/PathologyRecords/server/lib/jobs.js:616

*summary*: The steal ranking reads running_json from the holder's last heartbeat, which can be up to 30 s old. Coverage is unaffected (first report wins), but the waste is large at every exit's endgame and in the campaign tail. It also creates the same-job-twice-in-one-batch condition of the next finding.

*evidence*: jobs.js:500: running_json is written only by heartbeat (`q.setRunning.run(...)`). 616 `const rank = j => [runningOf.get(j.client_token).has(j.id) ? 1 : 0, -heldBy.get(j.client_token), -j.leased_at];`. 622-623 `const isRunning = runningOf.get(j.client_token).has(j.id); if (isRunning && tookRunning) break;`. stealJob (268) does not touch running_json. volunteer.py:508 `self.queue.extend(good)` has no de-duplication against running or queued ids. *expected_gain*: Removes an estimated 20-40 CPU-h of duplicate runs per campaign of this size and the concurrent same-client runs.

## Source report F168 (algo-collab)
**Endgame work stealing takes running jobs, ping-pongs them between clients and kills the previous holder's progress** at /Users/george/PathologyRecords/server/lib/jobs.js:616

*summary*: When the pool is empty, lease() re-leases jobs held by other clients, newest lease first. The holder's running list is up to 30 s stale, so a job that was just stolen is ranked as 'queued' and becomes the first candidate for the next thief. The previous holder then gets it in `drop` and SIGKILLs its worker. The result is many concurrent copies of the few remaining jobs, lost progress, and a later finish.

*failure_scenario*: The last 25 minutes of campaign #1. With the pool empty, every idle client's leaser (every ~10 s) steals. Job X moves A->B->C; each thief's running_json does not list X until its next heartbeat, and X has the newest leased_at, so it is re-stolen straight away. Holders learn about it only at their 30 s heartbeat and kill their copy, or keep running if they have re-stolen X in the meantime. In the worst case, job 141048 was 1,450 s into a monolithic 1,800 s run on MbObJu (it would have finished at +39,764) when it was stolen and split in 120 s windows by others. That split report won, and the split subtree (1,104 nodes, +16.7% states) finished at +40,514: the campaign's last completion came 750 s later than if the job had been left alone.

*evidence*: jobs.js:267 `... ORDER BY j.leased_at DESC LIMIT 5000`; :616 `const rank = j => [runningOf.get(j.client_token).has(j.id) ? 1 : 0, -heldBy.get(j.client_token), -j.leased_at];` (running_json is only written by heartbeat, :500); :533 `else drop.push(id);`; volunteer.py:1207-1210 `if s.job and s.job["id"] in drop and s.proc: ... self._signal(s, signal.SIGKILL)`. Snapshot: 1,411 'dup' reports carry 31.64 CPU-h (first node only), and 1,090 of them fall between +36,000 and +43,200 s. From +39,000 s to the end, 7.16 CPU-h of useful work remained while 17.83 CPU-h of duplicate reports were produced. Useful busy workers per 5 min: 50.6, 40.7, 24.5, 12.6, 3.6, 4.1, 0.5. Job 167664 was reported 41 times by 4 clients within 175 s. The 141048 reports: MbObJu exhausted in 1,792 s (10.02M states, best 117), reported at +39,764 as dup; RtSso4's split at +39,549 won.

*suggested_fix*: Replace running-job stealing with split-on-demand. When a lease finds the pool empty, pick running jobs of other clients, the longest-running first, and return them to their holder in the next heartbeat as `split_now:[ids]`. The holder SIGINTs that worker, which gives the exact REMAINING (already tested by test_split_exact's SIGINT mode). It then ends the window with no absorption and reports at once. While the pool is empty, the server can shorten heartbeat_s to about 10 s. Keep stealing only for jobs that are really queued: treat a job as queued only if leased_at < the holder's last heartbeat time, never steal a job leased less than 2 x heartbeat_s ago, never steal back from the client that just took it, and prefer holders whose last_seen is stale. Do not put stolen-running ids into the old holder's drop list; first report wins then really settles it.

*expected_gain*: About 20-30 CPU-h per campaign of this size (3-5% of 602 CPU-h: 31.6 CPU-h of duplicate reports, 17.8 of them in the last 25 min, plus unrecorded killed runs). About 15-20 min of wall clock: the last 25 min held only 7.16 CPU-h of real work, about 7 min for 65 workers. The waste grows with the number of idle thieves (160 workers next time).

## Verifier (CONFIRMED, high, refuted=False)
The premise matches the code. With the current client, the consequence is worse than the reported waste: the campaign tail can livelock.

1. Stale "running" view (/Users/george/PathologyRecords/server/lib/jobs.js)
- running_json is written only by heartbeat, at line 500: `if (Array.isArray(body.running)) q.setRunning.run(...)`.
- Neither the lease grant (leaseJob) nor stealJob (line 268) touches it. Every job granted or stolen since the holder's last heartbeat therefore ranks as queued: rank 0 at line 616, `const rank = j => [runningOf.get(j.client_token).has(j.id) ? 1 : 0, -heldBy.get(j.client_token), -j.leased_at];`. The heartbeat is 30 s (volunteer.py:41, CLIENT_PARAMS heartbeat_s 30).

2. Every request with an empty pool steals, and usually takes many jobs
- The steal runs whenever `need > 0 && jobs.length === 0` (line 608).
- Queued-looking jobs sort first and never set tookRunning. The only cap is line 623, `if (isRunning && tookRunning) break;`. So one request takes up to `need` just-moved jobs plus one running job.
- need = min(idle*ceil(300/mean_job_s), cap) minus the queue, which is about 30-45 for a 15-idle client.
- The -heldBy then newest-lease order puts the jobs just granted or stolen at the front.
- Each idle client asks every batch_interval_s = 10 s (volunteer.py:454-470). With 10 clients that is about 1 request per second.

3. Victims are killed and there is no dedupe
- heartbeat puts any held id that is no longer ours into `drop` (jobs.js:533, `else drop.push(id);`). The client SIGKILLs that worker and abandons the window with no report (volunteer.py:1202-1214, then run_window 634-638).
- leasedOthers (line 267, `j.client_token != ?`) excludes only jobs the requester holds right now. A client can be handed back a job it is still running, and volunteer.py:508 `self.queue.extend(good)` has no id dedupe, so it starts a second copy.

4. Snapshot confirms the bouncing
- Job 167664 has 1 split report plus 40 dups from 4 clients between +40514 and +40689. Start times (received minus elapsed) show a new copy about every 3 s.
- The same client reported the same job twice in the same second: RtSso4 at +40542, HuO58q at +40574, MbObJu at +40602.
- Job 141048: MbObJu's 1,792 s monolithic run was reported as a dup after RtSso4's 120 s split, which won at +39549.
- Dup CPU (first node only): 31.6 CPU-h in total, 1,090 dup reports in hours 10-11, and 15.86 CPU-h dup vs 6.81 CPU-h useful between +39000 and +40600.

5. Why campaign #1 still finished, and why the next one might not
- In the snapshot, copies of already-finished jobs ran 95-120 s past the finish (for example MbObJu's report at +40613 with 94 s elapsed). So campaign #1's clients or server did not kill on drop.
- The drop-kill client (commit 3829d5c, 2026-09-24 03:09 UTC, about +5 h into the campaign) is what volunteers will run next time.
- I ran an in-memory simulation of exactly these rules (lease rules, steal ranking, 30 s heartbeat with drop-kill, 10 s leaser). Tail job lengths were lognormal with 120 s windows and splits, and the client mixes were campaign #1's [15,12,10,32] and 10x16.
  - Current policy with drop-kill: the last job never finished within 6000 s in any seed, with thousands of copies started and killed (17-45 CPU-h burned against 2.6-3.4 CPU-h of useful work).
  - Current policy without kill (the campaign #1 regime): it finishes, but uses 3-7x the useful CPU.
  - Fixed policy (below): it finishes 20-45% sooner than no stealing, with about 0-2% duplicates.
- The mechanism for the last job is simple. It is the only candidate, so every other client's request takes it. A copy survives only if its client holds the lease at each of its heartbeats, which is about (1/N)^4 for a 120 s window with N clients. That is roughly 1e-4 for N = 10, so each critical-path window would take hours. This is a campaign stall (high), not only waste.

6. Exactness and gain
- No exactness risk: coverage is decided by first-report-wins reports (applyReport 747-771), and the fixes change only who runs what.
- For the old-client regime my estimate is about 15-30 CPU-h and 10-15 min per campaign of this size. The CPU is mostly otherwise-idle volunteer time.
- With current clients, the fix is what lets the tail finish without the owner stepping in. Worth doing before the next campaign.

*fix notes*: Server fixes (/Users/george/PathologyRecords/server/lib/jobs.js):

1. Treat a job as "possibly running" until the holder heartbeats after the grant.
- Add a clients.running_at column, set together with running_json at line 500.
- A job counts as running if it is in running_json OR `j.leased_at >= holder.running_at`.
- clients.last_seen is not usable for this, because the lease path updates it too (seenClient).

2. Minimum lease age: never steal a job whose leased_at is less than 2*heartbeat_s old. This stops the ping-pong.

3. Only steal genuinely queued jobs.
- Do not steal jobs that are running on a holder whose last_seen is within 2*heartbeat_s. Steal running jobs only from stale holders.
- Replace running-job theft with split-on-demand. The heartbeat response carries `split_now:[ids]` for the longest-running jobs of live holders when the pool is empty.
- The client already has the graceful path: _send_stop at volunteer.py:1027-1036 sends SIGINT only after the worker's first line, which also avoids the handler-install race. The SIGINT REMAINING path is covered by test_split_exact's SIGINT mode.
- The client should end that window with no absorption and report at once.
- Optionally lower heartbeat_s while the pool is empty.

4. Never grant a job id that is in the requester's own last running_json or holding list.

5. Keep drop-kill only for jobs that are finished, or whose lease lapsed and was re-issued. With the change in 3, that is the only way a live holder loses a job.

Client fix (volunteer.py:508): ignore leased ids already in a slot or in the queue.
