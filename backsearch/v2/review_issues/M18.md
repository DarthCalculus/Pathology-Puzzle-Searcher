# M18: The absorption pass probes the shallowest (largest) REMAINING entries first, against its comment, and wastes the 120 s budget

verdict CONFIRMED, severity medium, category algorithmic
locations: volunteer.py:648 (reversed(open_seeds)); REMAINING order (cursor, then stack top/deepest down to bottom/shallowest); DESIGN §7.3

## Summary
Nodes are appended in REMAINING order (cursor, then the stack from deepest to shallowest), so `reversed(open_seeds)` probes the shallowest, largest subtrees first. Each of those burns its full 10 s probe, usually splits, and its work is discarded. The 120 s budget runs out before the cheap deep entries are reached, so those become separate trivial server jobs, which defeats DESIGN §7.3.

*suggested_fix*: Probe open seeds deepest first (sort by token count, descending) and stop after the first probe that splits, because the siblings still to come are shallower and larger. *expected_gain*: About 16-21 worker-h per campaign (3-3.5% of CPU), about 35k fewer server jobs (roughly 70% of the 52k leased), and an open pool that really holds only jobs of 10 s or more, which makes the count-based ramp threshold meaningful.

## Verifier (CONFIRMED, medium, refuted=False)
**The premise holds in the code.**
- backsearch.c:4326-4329 `print_remaining` prints the cursor first, then `for (long long i = g_q_tail - 1; i >= 0; i--)`, which is the stack from the top (deepest) down to the bottom (shallowest).
- volunteer.py:628-631 appends the children to `nodes` in that REMAINING order.
- volunteer.py:641 builds `open_seeds` in `nodes` order, and :648 `for seed in reversed(open_seeds):     # most recent (deepest) first` then probes the bottom of the stack (the shallowest, largest subtrees) first. The comment is wrong.
- A probe that splits is dropped as open (:669-673), losing its 10 s.
- In practice a window has one split: J runs until the deadline, splits, and the loop breaks. So "reversed" really does mean shallow-first over the whole child list.

**The snapshot confirms it.** Server ids follow report node order (jobs.js:788-794), and job depth is the token count.
- Children grouped by REMAINING position (deep/cursor end to shallow end) in 1800 s windows: handed-back children under 10 s go 5,236, 4,801, 4,276, 2,671, 910. The absorbed count rises toward the shallow end: 2,484 to 3,764.
- The average handed-back cost at the shallow end is 251 s; at the deep end it is 0.1 s.
- Job 1019's children in id order go from +31 to +1 relative tokens; the last ones cost 1,033 s, 1,800 s (split), 30 s and 1,800 s (split).
- Overall, 48,336 non-root leased jobs finished done. Of these, 39,007 took under 10 s and 35,379 under 1 s.

**Own simulation** (scratchpad/review/absorb_sim.py, absorb_sim2.py). It replays each split window's children with their recorded costs, a 120 s budget, a 10 s probe and 0.25 s of per-run overhead.
- Current order reproduces the snapshot almost exactly: 103,393 absorbed simulated vs 103,613 actual, and 38,255 handed back under 10 s simulated vs 38,136 actual. That validates the model.
- Deep-first order (token count descending) leaves only about 4,300 handed-back children under 10 s. That is about 34k fewer trivial leased jobs per campaign #1-sized run: about 70% of the ~51k leased jobs and about 20% of all 167k jobs.
- Failed-probe waste only drops from 25.9 to 22.3 CPU-h, because the leftover budget still goes to shallow probes that split.

**My estimate of the gain:**
- The ordering fix alone saves about 34k lease/report cycles. The report table would shrink from about 54k to about 20k rows. The open-pool count would become meaningful for the §7.4 ramp test (`open < 3*workersActive`), which trivial jobs currently inflate.
- It also saves about 3.6 CPU-h. It does not save 16-21 h.
- More CPU saving needs a second change:
  - deep-first with `absorb_probe_s` 3 s: waste about 14 h, about 220 children under 1 s and about 6.3k under 10 s left;
  - deep-first with 5 s: waste 18.7 h;
  - deep-first, 10 s, stopping after 2 consecutive failed probes: waste 14.8 h, 10.4k under 10 s left.
- That is 1-2% of campaign CPU on top of the ordering fix.

**F170's "better still" idea should not be adopted as stated.** It keeps each failed probe as a split node with its REMAINING children. About 8k failed probes x about 80 REMAINING entries (the snapshot averages 79.6 children per split) would add hundreds of thousands of mostly trivial open jobs. It would also push trees toward the MAX_NODES=5000 truncation at volunteer.py:681-684. That path truncates `nodes[:MAX_NODES]` and could separate a split node from its children. It cannot happen today (the largest report was 268 nodes), but it would become reachable.

**Exactness risk of the ordering fix: none.** Only the probe iteration order changes. Every probed seed ends up either done with a full exhausted SUMMARY or open for the server, and the level of a split probe is still kept. `nodes`, and with it the parent-before-child report order, stays unchanged.

**Coverage:** test_client.sh only asserts that at least one child was absorbed and that absorbed children were never leased. It does not check which children are absorbed, so no test covers the order. That is why this went unnoticed.

*fix notes*: 1. volunteer.py:648: iterate `for seed in sorted(open_seeds, key=lambda s: s.count(","), reverse=True):`. Python's sort is stable even with reverse=True, so the cursor still comes first among equal depths. Fix the comment. Do not reorder `nodes`, because the report needs the parent-before-child order.
2. Optionally cut failed-probe waste. Either lower `absorb_probe_s` to 3-5 s in the server CLIENT_PARAMS (jobs.js:54; it can also be set per campaign), or stop the pass after 2-3 consecutive probes that split. Deep-first order makes the remaining siblings shallower and costlier. Expect about 7-12 CPU-h saved at campaign-#1 scale, on top of about 3.6 h from the ordering fix.
3. Do not turn failed probes into split nodes unless the extra open children are bounded. That would add about 80 open children per probe and approach MAX_NODES.
4. Update DESIGN §7.3 to say deep-first order. Add a test_client.sh case in which fake_worker's shallow children cost more than the probe and the deep children are trivial, with absorb_total_s smaller than the sum of the shallow probes. Assert that every deep child arrives done and none is leased. Tests today only check that at least one child was absorbed.
