# M21: `holding` omits finished jobs whose reports are pending or in the outbox, so heartbeats release them for re-lease while the report is in flight

verdict CONFIRMED, severity low, category protocol
locations: volunteer.py:1163 (holding = running + queued ids); self.pending (batched every 10 s); outbox backoff (up to 300 s); take_job popleft -> run_window slot.job gap; jobs.js:514 (release-by-omission)

## Summary
holding contains only running and queued ids. A job whose window has finished waits in self.pending for up to 10 s, or in the outbox for minutes, and is not listed during that time. A heartbeat in that gap releases its lease (older than 120 s), and another client may lease and re-run the job until the late report lands and triggers `drop`. The short gap between take_job's popleft and run_window setting slot.job has the same effect.

## Source report F84 (server-scheduling)
**Release-by-omission also releases finished jobs whose report has not been delivered yet** at /Users/george/PathologyRecords/server/lib/jobs.js:514

*summary*: `holding` is the slots' jobs plus the queue. A finished job waits in `pending` for up to batch_interval_s (10 s), or in the outbox for up to 300 s of backoff, and is not listed during that time. A heartbeat in that gap reopens it, and another client may re-run it. The same happens in the short gap between take_job's popleft and run_window setting slot.job.

*failure_scenario*: J (leased 20 minutes ago) finishes and its report sits in pending. The next heartbeat omits J, so the server reopens it. At the tail an idle client leases and starts it at once. A's report then arrives and wins (J is open, so it is accepted), and B's copy is killed at its next heartbeat. After a network glitch, every job in the outbox (retried with up to 300 s backoff) is released and re-run meanwhile. There is no coverage loss, only waste, and it feeds the tail thrash.

*evidence*: volunteer.py 695-700: `slot.job = None` ... `self.queue_report(report)`. Sender 887-897 waits until batch_interval_s has passed since the last send. Outbox: `min(300.0, 2.0 ** attempts)`. 1162-1163: `holding = running + [j["id"] for j in self.queue]`. jobs.js 514 releases anything not in holding that is older than 120 s.

*suggested_fix*: Have the client include the ids of pending and outbox reports in `holding` until the server acknowledges them, or have the server skip release for jobs whose report was received in the last few minutes.

## Source report F195 (robustness-ops)
**Finished but not-yet-sent reports are missing from `holding`, so their jobs are released and handed out again** at /Users/george/Documents/Claude Projects/project45/backsearch/v2/volunteer.py:1163

*summary*: holding contains only the running and queued jobs. A job whose window finished has slot.job cleared before its report is batched (every 10 s) or while it waits in the outbox (backoff up to 300 s). If a heartbeat falls in that gap, the server releases the job and another client may lease it and start work that is killed once the late report lands.

*failure_scenario*: With batch_interval 10 s and heartbeat 30 s, about one window in three is released before its report arrives. The deepest-first lease order hands it to another client, which spawns a worker and kills it on its next heartbeat (`drop`, up to 30 s later). After a server outage or a client restart, outboxed reports wait up to 300 s of backoff while run() sends a heartbeat with an empty holding first. Every outboxed job is then re-leased, and others burn up to 5 minutes each on work that is already done.

*evidence*: volunteer.py:695 `slot.job = None` runs before 700 `self.queue_report(report)`. volunteer.py:1162-1163 `running = [s.job["id"] for s in self.slots if s.job]; holding = running + [j["id"] for j in self.queue]`. volunteer.py:965 `"next_try": time.time() + min(300.0, 2.0 ** attempts)`. volunteer.py:1367-1369: heartbeat() runs before flush_outbox(), and flush_outbox skips entries whose next_try is in the future. jobs.js:514 release.

*suggested_fix*: Add the job ids of `pending` and of outbox files to `holding`. After any successful request following failures, flush the outbox at once, ignoring next_try.

## Verifier (CONFIRMED, low, refuted=False)
Mechanism confirmed. holding at volunteer.py:1161-1163 lists only slot jobs and queued jobs. run_window sets `slot.job = None` (line 695) before `self.queue_report(report)` (line 700). jobs.js:514 reopens any lease not in holding whose leased_at is more than 120 s old. run() calls heartbeat() (1367) before flush_outbox() (1369), and flush skips entries whose next_try lies in the future. Coverage is not at risk: first-report-wins (applyReport, jobs.js ~747-771), and the loser receives `drop` at its next heartbeat (jobs.js:527-533) and SIGKILLs the copy. The steady-state rates in the source reports ('1 in 6', 'one in three') are overstated. sender_loop (889-897) sends as soon as `time.time() - last_send_at >= batch_interval_s`, and last_send_at changes only when a batch goes out. With 16 workers and 1800 s windows, a client finishes a window about every 110 s. A report therefore usually waits at most 0.5 s (send_event.wait(0.5)) plus the POST round trip. So the gap is about 1 s: a 30 s heartbeat hits it for roughly 3% of windows. Waste then follows only if another client leases that exact job within that second, which is unlikely with a large deepest-first pool; at the tail the CPUs that take it are idle anyway. The take_job popleft to slot.job gap is two adjacent lock sections (microseconds), which is negligible. The case that does cost CPU is the outbox. After a server outage or network loss of several minutes, backoff reaches `min(300, 2**attempts)` (line 965) while heartbeats succeed again. Every outboxed job is then released and may be re-run by others for up to about 300 s plus 30 s. That is bounded at a few CPU-hours per long outage across 10 clients, which is waste only. Low.

*fix notes*: Add to holding the job ids of self.pending, the in-flight batch and the outbox files, but cap it: only while the report is younger than lease_s, or while attempts < N. Otherwise a batch the server keeps failing with a transient 5xx, which is retried forever, would keep its job leased indefinitely and could stall the campaign tail. When a request succeeds after failures (api.connected turns true), flush the outbox at once and ignore next_try. Optionally assign slot.job inside take_job under the same lock as the popleft.
