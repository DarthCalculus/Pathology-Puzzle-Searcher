## 1. Verdict

### Campaign #1 (5x5, at most 3 holes; published 127/149/112/138/115/80)

Nothing found shows that a published maximum is wrong. **Exhaustiveness is not fully proven**, for one reason that matters and two that are probabilistic.

1. **Silent capacity prunes (C1 below): cannot be ruled out.**
   - Mechanism: when the pending queue of a single shortcut check reaches 1,048,576 entries, the solver returns -2 (`sokoban_bfs.c:1734`, `if (hs->pq_count >= HP64_SIZE) ... return -2;`).
   - The generator then prunes that state and its whole subtree as if a shortcut had been proven (`backsearch.c:2218`, `if (x >= 0 || x == -2 || x == -3 || x == -4) { g_pruned_short++; ... return; }`).
   - It leaves no trace. The prune is counted together with real shortcuts, SUMMARY has no field for it, and the job still reports "exhausted".
   - The prunes are biased the wrong way. The biggest solves are the exhaustive ones, i.e. accepted states, because a solve that finds a shortcut stops early. So a -2 is more likely to hit a state that would have been valid.
   - What the data does show:
     - No job root was lost this way: 0 of 164,661 finished jobs have accepted = 0.
     - The one profiled sample job peaked at about 100k popped states per check, roughly 10x below the cap.
     - -3 and -4 cannot occur in the campaign configuration (§6).
2. **64-bit hash identity (M6, M7).**
   - Generator dedup and the multi-start label table identify states by a 64-bit hash alone.
   - Expected false dedup prunes over campaign #1: about 0.01.
   - The chance that one of them removed a level of maximal length is far smaller; the verifier estimates about 1e-10.
3. **Prune soundness is argued but not tested against an oracle (M89).** Five reviewers re-derived every prune under the campaign flags and found each one sound. No test compares the worker with an independent enumerator.

**What is independently established:**
- **Roots.**
  - An independent Python lister reproduced all 2,761 layer-4 roots exactly (42/355/233/810/972/349, same seed strings) under the campaign flags.
  - Wrong flags give different root counts: 2 holes 2,624; 4 holes 2,767; no transit 919; block-on-exit 2,779.
  - No layer-4 root contains a walk onto a committed cell, and none can: brute force on 5x5, 4x6, 6x6, 3x3 and 2x5 finds the first such walk at step 5. So M1 did not affect campaign #1.
- **Coverage.** The audit, recomputed offline, is clean:
  - every child extends its split parent;
  - depth equals the token count;
  - there are no duplicate seeds;
  - all roots are covered;
  - there is one whitelisted hash, and it equals the sha256 of the current sources.
- **Limits never reached, and no failure mode fired:**
  - max 268 nodes per report (cap 5,000: M15);
  - deepest seed 128 tokens (cap 250: M2);
  - 0 bad_tree/bad_level reports;
  - 0 done jobs with 0 states (M48);
  - 0 rows with a level but no best (M33; re-checked today);
  - no solver hang (M4).
- **Champions and determinism.**
  - All six champions were re-solved by the site's solver6 to `verified_moves = best`.
  - 1,009 of 1,009 fingerprint duplicates matched across machines, including jobs with dedup evictions.

**How to check cheaply** (the owner decides what to do with the result):
- **Tier 1, about 1 CPU-h.**
  - Build a private, non-whitelisted copy of the current worker that counts -2/-3 returns and records the maximum `pq_count` per run, both printed in SUMMARY. This is about 10 lines, at `backsearch.c:2218` and the `pq_count` sites.
  - Run it on the ~25 campaign-#1 job seeds with the highest cost per state (for example jobs 27048, 27049 and 16706), 120 s each.
  - A zero counter with a max `pq_count` well under 1,048,576 makes -2 implausible for this search class. A non-zero counter gives the exact path to re-search.
- **Tier 2, definitive:** a complete re-run with the counter. That costs about 600 CPU-h, or about 335 CPU-h for exit 1 alone, which holds the 149. The next release worker (C1 fix) would produce this as a by-product if the owner re-runs campaign #1's class.
- **Split-test logs:** check the lead's two test_split_exact logs for a non-zero `extra` count (M87). A non-zero count would point at a dedup soundness bug.
- **Hash collisions:** there is no cheap check. The risk is negligible; N13 removes it for future runs.

### Readiness for campaign #2: NOT READY

**Blocking, whatever the parameters:**
- **C1 and H1.** The silent capacity prune and the solver's infinite retry both grow with solve size, which is exactly what more holes or a bigger grid increases.
- **H4 (M16), which will fire on day one.** Seeding #2 with `--close-others` closes #1. Every stored token then gets HTTP 410, which the client treats as "server unreachable" and retries forever, so every returning volunteer stalls.

## 2. Must fix before the next campaign

### C1 (M3 + N9): solver capacity results are pruned as proven shortcuts. CONFIRMED, critical

**Where**
- `backsearch.c:2218` (try_successor_x), `:1840` (NN flush; it also accepts -3, see N21), `:2407` (bulk multi-solve), `:2647`, `:4401` (a replayed root gets a fresh solve with no reference table).
- `sokoban_bfs.c:1734/1245/2060/2470/2640`: the -2 returns (`HP64_SIZE = 1<<20` at `:939`).
- `sokoban_bfs.h`: its own contract says -2/-3 must be treated as fatal.

**What goes wrong**
- A state whose shortcut status is unknown is dropped together with its subtree.
- It was already dedup-inserted at `:2163-2167`, so later visits at the same or greater depth are suppressed too.
- Neither SUMMARY, the client, the audit nor the fingerprints can see it.
- A parent decides its children with smaller reference-table or bulk solves, while a replayed REMAINING root gets a fresh, reference-free solve. The cap can therefore fire in one split layout and not in another.
- The cap itself is artificial: the Dial buckets grow with realloc.

**Fix (N9 design)**
- One `classify(x)` helper, used at every consumer:
  - `x >= 0`: prune;
  - `-1`: accept;
  - `-2`, `-3` and the new `-5` (H1): UNKNOWN.
- On UNKNOWN:
  - push the state with an `unverified` flag that is outside the dedup key and the path;
  - do not count it as valid and do not update best or the win counters;
  - never export a parent table from it (export only on -1; inheriting an ancestor's table stays sound);
  - emit it as a candidate `(depth, level code, path)` if it has no block on the exit.
- Add a total and per-cause counters to SUMMARY. Past a budget, end the run with status `unresolved_overflow` and a non-zero exit code.
- Server:
  - whitelist the new fields;
  - an exit is exact only when every candidate deeper than its verified best has been resolved offline, by solver6 or a large-table check.
- The invariant becomes V ⊆ T ⊆ V ∪ U, where V are the verified levels, U the unverified candidates and T the true level set. Update DESIGN §1 and §2.
- Lockstep: the new fields and status need the version gate (N16).

### C2 (M1 + N11): root listing and job replay use different tree semantics. CONFIRMED, critical, latent at K <= 4

**Where**
- `backsearch.c:196`/`:4371`: `g_bulk_walk_active` is set only in run_exit_search, never in run_estimate or `--list-layer` (`:4061-4228`).
- `:2060-2074`: replay folds a trailing committed-walk run into a bulk edge (`seed.bulk_walk = 1`).
- `:2476`/`:2526`: expand() skips committed walk-backs of a bulk node.

**What goes wrong**
- A root listed with bulk walk-back off can end in a committed walk.
- A job replays that root as a bulk child and skips its committed walk-backs.
- Those cells are bulk siblings of a node above the layer, which no job owns, so their subtrees are never searched.
- A committed walk needs at least 5 backward steps on any grid, so K <= 4 is safe. The lead's layer-8 run showed the loss.
- DESIGN §6 and the v2_seed example still say layer 8 (N6/N20).

**Fix**
- Now:
  - `--list-layer` refuses K >= 5 while bulk walk-back is on.
  - While printing, replay each root with the `:2060` predicate and exit 2 if any token is a committed walk.
  - Print `LAYERINFO K bulk SRC_HASH flags`.
  - v2_seed rejects a layer above 4, roots whose token count is not K, and a LAYERINFO that does not match `plan.extra` or the whitelist.
- Later, only if deeper layers are needed: list with bulk semantics. That means setting `g_bulk_walk_active` in run_estimate, emitting every child with depth >= K, and widening `g_probe_buf` beyond 64 (N19).

### C3 (M15): the client truncates tree reports at 5,000 nodes but still reports the split. CONFIRMED, critical, latent (about 18x headroom on 5x5)

**Where**
- `v2/volunteer.py:680-684`: `"nodes": [self._clean_node(n) for n in nodes[:MAX_NODES]]`, followed by a log line saying "the rest stay open on the server", which is false.
- `jobs.js` parseTree: a split only needs at least one child.

**What goes wrong**
- The cut drops the bottom of the DFS stack, which holds the shallowest and largest pending subtrees.
- The server counts the parent as covered once the listed children finish, and the audit reports clean.
- A bigger grid or a longer window can reach the cap.

**Fix (N12 fold-back)**
- Never cut a split's child list.
- Demote the deepest split nodes to open (drop their descendants, keep any levels) until the tree fits the node and byte caps.
- If J's own REMAINING alone exceeds the cap, hand the job back untouched and log loudly.
- Optionally, let the server accept a split in several parts.

### H1 (M4 + N10 + N3): infinite retry when the 16M-slot table fills. CONFIRMED, high

**Where**
- `sokoban_bfs.c:1751-1754` (solve_push64_cutoff) and `:1262-1265` (solve_push64, the end-of-run verify of every job's best): `} else if (hs->overflow) { use_big = 1; goto retry; }`.

**What goes wrong**
- The retry runs even when the solve is already on the big table.
- `hsp64_select` rebuilds the same limit of 14,260,633 inserts (`:1020`), so the deterministic solve overflows again, forever.
- No split or stop check runs inside a solve, so the worker never prints REMAINING or SUMMARY. The client keeps renewing the lease, and the core stays pinned until the client is stopped.
- The uncapped, reference-free final verify is the most exposed site, and it can hang a job whose search had already finished.
- It never fired in campaign #1.

**Fix**
- `if (use_big) return -5;` before the retry, at both sites. -5 is UNKNOWN (C1). The 128-bit solvers have no fill limit.
- In v2 mode, bound the final verify: use a cutoff solve at best-2, or skip it after a split.
- Client: a per-run watchdog. If no STATUS arrives for about 3x the status interval plus split_after, send SIGINT, then SIGKILL, and report a failure.

### H2 (M2 + N12 + M51): paths deeper than 250 tokens are silently truncated. CONFIRMED, high, latent (campaign #1 max 128)

**Where**
- `backsearch.c:227` (`PATH_TOK_MAX 250`), `:456-466` (`uint8_t plen`; `if (s->plen >= PATH_TOK_MAX) return 0;`). The callers at `:1961/1987/2005/2030/2250/2371` ignore the return value.
- `parse_seed_path` accepts 1024 tokens.
- `v2/volunteer.py:771/783-790/826-834`: bad or overlong lines are dropped, and the run is still reported as a split.
- `jobs.js` SEED_MAX_TOKENS is 1024.

**What goes wrong**
- A deeper node keeps a 250-token prefix, so REMAINING names an ancestor or a bulk sibling.
- The client drops the non-extending line and reports the split with fewer children.
- The result is silent subtree loss, or a subtree searched twice, at record depths on bigger grids. 6x6 very likely exceeds 250.

**Fix**
- A path_push failure sets a flag. The DFS then ends with status `path_overflow`, prints no REMAINING, and exits non-zero.
- Reject `--seed-path` longer than PATH_TOK_MAX with status `bad_seed`.
- Client: any malformed, non-extending or overlong REMAINING line voids the run (the node stays open). Never report a partial split.
- `--version` prints LIMITS, so the server can cap SEED_MAX_TOKENS to them.
- Widen `plen` to uint16_t before a deep grid.

### H3 (M45): 128-bit state packing overflows with no fit check. CONFIRMED, high, latent (5x5 needs at most 108 bits)

**Where**
- `sokoban_bfs.c:2127-2132` (pack128).
- `:2674/2681`: the dispatcher checks only the 64-bit limit.
- Unpack sites at `:2155/2166/2375/2391/2554`.

**What goes wrong**
- At 6 bits per cell (6x6, 7x7), 20 blocks plus 3 holes need 129 bits.
- The shifts then lose bits or become UB. States alias in the exact-key table and holes look filled, so shortcut checks answer wrongly in both directions.

**Fix**
- At startup, clamp `g_max_blocks` loudly to the largest nb with `b*(1+nb)+nh <= 128`, or refuse to start.
- In the solver, abort (never prune) on a state that does not fit.

### H4 (M16): tokens of a closed campaign get HTTP 410 and the client never re-registers. CONFIRMED, high

**Where**
- `v2/volunteer.py:342-352`: `if e.status in (401, 403, 404): ... else: self.log("cannot reach the server yet ...")` with a 10 s retry loop.
- The heartbeat stops only on 401/403, and the leaser retries.
- `jobs.js:331-339`: requireClient returns `fail(410, 'campaign_closed')`.
- `tools/v2_seed.js:60-68`: `--close-others` closes #1.

**What goes wrong**
- Seeding #2 makes every stored campaign-1 token return 410.
- Returning volunteers loop at startup; running clients idle, and their reports go to `rejected/`.
- Only deleting `~/.pathology_volunteer.json` or running with `--reregister` gets them out, and nothing tells them so.

**Fix**
- Treat 410 like 403/404 in register(), the heartbeat and the leaser: drop the token and register again. register() already waits on 404 `no_campaign`.
- Add a 410 case to fake_server, test_client and the gate tests.
- Ship this before seeding #2.

### H5 (M17): several clients on one machine share one token and release each other's jobs. CONFIRMED, high

**Where**
- `v2/volunteer.py:35`: one token file per HOME.
- `:322/:386`: tokens are keyed only by server URL.
- `v2/README.md:39-40` recommends running several clients.
- `jobs.js:514`: release by omission.

**What goes wrong**
- Each instance's heartbeat releases the other instance's running and queued jobs every 30 s.
- Other clients lease them, and the owning instance gets `drop` and SIGKILLs its workers.
- The same happens with NFS home directories.

**Fix**
- Give each process its own identity: key the token by server plus outbox path, and hold an exclusive lock file.
- Alternatively, send a per-process instance id and have release by omission touch only that instance's leases.
- Correct the README.

### H6 (M27): tail work stealing thrashes. CONFIRMED, high

**Where**
- `jobs.js:616`: `rank = j => [runningOf.get(j.client_token).has(j.id) ? 1 : 0, -heldBy..., -j.leased_at]`.
- `running_json` is written only by the heartbeat (`:500`); steal and lease at `:268` and `:601-631`.
- `v2/volunteer.py:508`: grants are not deduped (M19).

**What goes wrong**
- A job granted or stolen within the holder's last 30 s ranks as "queued", so it is the first zero-cost victim again.
- Jobs bounce about every 10 s and victims are SIGKILLed. A client can even be handed back a job it is still running.
- Snapshot: 31.6 CPU-h of duplicate reports, 17.8 of them in the last 25 minutes.
- With drop-kill honoured everywhere, the tail can livelock.

**Fix**
- Store `running_at`, and treat any lease newer than it as running.
- Steal running jobs only from stale holders.
- Never grant a job listed in the requester's own last running or holding list.
- Limit re-steals per window.
- Dedupe grants in the client (M19).

### H7 (M28): `v2_hashes remove --reissue` re-searches nested subtrees about 4.4x and keeps stale bests. CONFIRMED, high

**Where**
- `jobs.js:313`, `:422-429` (reopenUnknownHashJobs) and resetJob at `:314-315`: rows are reset, but parent links and children are kept.

**What goes wrong**
- Every reset split node recreates a child set, while its old children are also reset.
- A state at tree level k is searched k+1 times, about 4.4x on campaign #1's tree shape.
- Probe rows created by the removed worker keep feeding `MAX(best)`.
- This is the documented recovery procedure after a worker fix (README:49, DESIGN §1.4).

**Fix**
- Reset only the topmost old-hash node on each path, and tombstone its descendants so treeOfExit excludes them.
- Clear `level_code`/`best` on rows created under the removed hash.
- Add a gate test.

### H8 (M30): status and audit are recomputed on the request path, and the cache stops caching at scale. CONFIRMED, high

**Where**
- `jobs.js:934-937`: `statusCache = { at: n, body: status() }` stamps the time before computing.
- `:538`: the heartbeat runs statusCached/auditExitCached inside BEGIN IMMEDIATE.
- `:892-930`, bestOfToken, and campaignsSummary, which re-audits every campaign each minute.

**What goes wrong**
- status() takes about 0.3 s at 167k rows (measured on the laptop).
- At about 10x the rows, a recompute takes 2 s or more, so the cache has already expired when it is stored.
- Every heartbeat and every 3 s dashboard poll then recomputes, which blocks the single Node event loop that also serves the public site.

**Fix**
- Compute status and audits on a timer, outside request handlers and outside the write transaction.
- Stamp the cache after the compute, and serve the stale copy while recomputing.
- Cache closed campaigns.
- Keep per-exit and per-name aggregates incrementally in applyReport.
- Add indexes `(campaign_id, exit, dup_of, id)` and `jobs(client_token, best DESC)`.

## 3. Should fix (medium)

Test gaps (M87-M91, M93, N18) are in §5. Scheduling and performance items (M18, M100/N2, M103, M107-M109) are in §4.

**Worker and build**
- **M9: a worker uses about 0.44-0.6 GB, not the ~200 MB the docs say.**
  - Measured: 438 MiB max RSS on a 45 s campaign job, and 882 MiB on an uncapped 6x6 run.
  - Causes: a 256 MiB big table and a 384 MiB 128-bit table, allocated lazily and never freed (`sokoban_bfs.c:1018`), plus 160 MiB of dedup tables (`backsearch.c:5581-5583`).
  - Fix: grow the tables on demand or madvise them after big solves; document the real peak; default `--workers` to at most RAM_GB/0.6.
- **M48: inherited `BS_DUMP_SEED` makes every run "exhausted" with 0 states** (`backsearch.c:4394-4399`), and `BS_TRACE_*` fill the disk.
  - Fix: the worker ignores these in protocol mode, the client strips `BS_*`, and the server rejects an exhausted run with states < 1.
- **M13: following the update advice leaves the old binary in use.**
  - `./build_pgo.sh` writes `./backsearch_worker` (and links libtorch if it is installed), while the client runs `./backsearch_worker_nt`.
  - The plain-cc fallback in `VOLUNTEERS.md:35` compiles without a SRC_HASH, so the client refuses it.
  - Fix: print `./build_pgo.sh -o backsearch_worker_nt --no-torch` (`v2/volunteer.py:293/400-401`, `build_pgo.sh:25`).
- **N6 / N20: the docs still prescribe root layer 8, the configuration C2 breaks.** This is in DESIGN §6 (`v2/DESIGN.md:223-224`), `tools/v2_seed.js:10` and `backsearch/README.md:235`. Change them to layer 4 and state the rule.

**Client** (all paths in `v2/volunteer.py` unless stated)
- **M14: a client that dies on SIGHUP or SIGKILL leaves its paused workers SIGSTOPped forever, holding RAM.** Workers run in their own session (`:743-745`), and only SIGINT/SIGTERM are handled (`:1352-1356`).
  - Fix: handle SIGHUP like Stop, clean up from a pidfile at startup, and have the worker exit when getppid() changes.
- **M19: lease grants are not deduped against queued or running jobs** (`:484-509`), so one client can run the same job in several slots. At least 10.5 CPU-h was measured.
- **M20 + N15 + M49: poison jobs are re-leased forever.**
  - A bad seed ends with status `time_cap` and exit code 0 (`backsearch.c:4386`); the client logs it as "no SUMMARY" and drops it (`:803-818`).
  - Rejected reports are forgotten (`:951`), the server reopens the job (`jobs.js:750-755`), and nothing counts attempts.
  - Fix: the worker reports `bad_seed` and exits 3; the client sends failure reports; the server keeps a fail_count and quarantines after N failures, and the audit lists those jobs as uncovered.
- **M23: a report's src_hash is only the first run's hash** (`:605/:657`), so runs from a binary rebuilt mid-window are reported under the old whitelisted hash.
  - Fix: void the run on a hash change, or run a private copy of the binary pinned at startup.

## 4. Algorithmic and performance improvements

Ranked by expected gain against risk. CPU figures are per campaign of campaign-#1 size (602 CPU-h). "(hash)" means the change alters SRC_HASH.

1. **Tail stealing fixes, M27 (server).**
   - Gain: 20-30 CPU-h (3-5%), and it removes the tail livelock.
   - Basis: 31.6 CPU-h of duplicate reports in the snapshot, 17.8 of them in the last 25 minutes.
   - Risk: none to exactness, because the first report wins.
2. **Absorption order, M18 (client).** Probe REMAINING deepest-first (`v2/volunteer.py:648`: `reversed(...)` currently probes the shallowest first), stop after the first probe that splits, and keep a split probe as a split node with its children.
   - Gain: 16-21 worker-h (3-3.5%) and about 35k fewer server jobs.
   - Basis: snapshot size distribution plus an absorption simulation.
   - Risk: low; exact by construction.
3. **Largest-first leasing across exits, M107 + M110 (server).** Store `est_s` per root from `--estimate-dump` at seeding (the per-layer estimates v2_seed currently ignores) and lease by it across exits, instead of "exit with the most open jobs, deepest first" (`jobs.js:272/275`).
   - Gain: removes most of the 25-35 minute tail (about 4% of wall clock) at no CPU cost.
   - Basis: critical-path trace.
   - Caveat from the verifier: depth ascending inside the current per-exit rule is worse.
4. **Client grant dedupe, M19.** At least 10.5 CPU-h, measured. No risk.
5. **Ramp and endgame policy, M109.**
   - Change: base the ramp threshold on pool work rather than job count; set the absorption budget to min(120, 0.1 x window); in the endgame, set split_after = clamp(pool work / idle workers, 15, 120) and send splits immediately.
   - Gain: about 10-14 worker-h (overlaps item 2); final chain steps drop from about 250 s to about 30-40 s.
   - Risk: low.
6. **Lease-ahead, M108 (client).** Hold at most about 2 x workers, or size requests by expected work, and choose split_after when the window starts.
   - Gain: takes the local-queue wait (about 900 s on average, up to 2.5 h) off critical paths.
7. **Growable solver tables, M103 (hash).**
   - Change: grow from 64K to 16M slots instead of restarting on a 256 MB table; export parent tables from large solves; raise the MSLab cap.
   - Gain: unmeasured. If 10-30% of solver time is in solves above 32K inserts, 5-15% overall, plus about 200 MB less RSS.
   - Risk: medium. Gate it on solvebench, multitest and REF_CHECK.
8. **Skip the solver for dead-end children, M101 (hash).** When the new player cell's only neighbour is P (plus the listed conditions), the child provably has no shortcut.
   - Gain: 2-4% of solver time (verifier; the finder said 3-10%).
   - Risk: medium. Verify with a check build that still runs the solver.
9. **Hot-path micro-costs, M105 (hash).** A 24-byte heap entry (dropping the dead `used` field also removes the known UB), tail_width only when it is used, direct key indexing, and a key-only symmetry transform.
   - Gain: claimed 2-6% of solver time, about 4% at centre exits. The verifier considers this overstated.
10. **Fewer forced splits, M10.** Use long windows while the pool is deep, split on demand, and print periodic REMAINING checkpoints.
    - Gain: up to about 3.3% fewer states on subtrees split for no reason (35 natural pairs), and up to about 13 worker-h of absorption.
    - Trade-off: a crash loses more work.
11. **Representative PGO training and native ISA flags, M40 (no hash change).**
    - Gain: 0-2% (verifier), 1-5% (finder).
    - Also make the build fail on an empty profile.
12. **Skip the memset of fresh dedup tables, M8 (hash).**
    - Gain: about 0.3% CPU, about 2x throughput on trivial jobs and absorption probes, and about 2.5 GB less RSS on a 16-worker client.
    - It also makes the test matrix cheaper: about 17 ms per process drops to about 2 ms.
13. **Cross-job transposition dedup, M106.**
    - Gain: 0.8%, measured at job roots.
    - Constraint: key it on (canonical key, depth, bulk) and use only server-acknowledged records; a live shared table would be unsound. Low priority.
14. **Mandatory-hole prune, M104.** The gain is unmeasured; 12 A/B runs gave identical counts. It needs a lazy closure table first; an A/B costs about 1 CPU-h.
15. **Wide-state fast path, M100 + N2 (hash).**
    - Gain: about 0 for 5x5 with at most 4 holes and for 4x6.
    - On 6x6, an estimated 45-77% of checks take the unoptimised 128-bit path (PLAUSIBLE). An uncapped 6x6 run measured 882 MiB RSS.
    - Only worth doing if 6x6 is planned; the alternative is `--num-blocks 9`.
16. **Fingerprint sampling, M32.** Give duplicates split_after of at least 2 x the original's elapsed time, and exclude them from steal and reclaim.
    - Gain: the same 2% budget verifies about 2% of states instead of 0.8%, and about 3 CPU-h of churn disappears.
    - The "sample proportional to elapsed" variant gains nothing.
17. **Persist lease, steal and absorption events, M111.** No direct gain, but it is the data needed to tune items 1-6.

Refuted as improvements: M5 (cursor re-listing; the gain is too small) and M102 (A* bound with an occupied exit; negligible).

## 6. Checked and refuted

**Refuted findings and sub-claims**
- **M5** REMAINING re-lists the already-expanded cursor with its children. True, but the subtrees only overlap and never leave a gap, and fixing it gains almost nothing.
- **M102** An A* bound that accounts for the occupied exit. Sound, but the gain is negligible.
- **M43** "Leases expire because split checks are coarse." Refuted: heartbeats renew leases. Only the window overrun stands.
- **M41** "An empty root seed stalls a campaign." Not reachable: v2_seed's readLayer rejects empty seeds.
- **N14(a)** "A stale last_exhaustive leaks across exits in multi-exit runs." Refuted: the end-of-exit verify solve clears it.
- **N19** "Probe-buffer overflow today." Refuted: at most 16 children per node with bulk walk-back off.
- **M87** "The evidence was thrown away." Refuted: the extra count is printed in the logs, so it can still be checked.
- **M32** "Sample duplicates proportional to elapsed time." Gains nothing at a fixed budget.
- **M107** "Depth ascending within the current per-exit order." Makes things worse; it helps only across exits.
- **M21** Steady-state release rates of 1 in 3 to 1 in 6 are overstated, because the sender sends as soon as a batch is ready.

**Suspected and found sound**
- **Dedup key.** Leaving wh/seg/adjflags/bulk_walk out of the dedup key is sound: every path-dependent prune proves a shortcut for the state itself.
- **REMAINING** is complete: the cursor plus the full stack. Nothing else holds pending work in campaign mode.
- **Seed replay** rebuilds nodes exactly, including multi-token bulk edges. The missing adjflags reset at P==exit is dead code, because the player stands on the exit only at depth 0.
- **Split and stop timing.**
  - A split never happens mid-expansion.
  - The stop flag is checked after every expansion.
  - The real worker installs its signal handler before printing SRC_HASH.
- **Symmetry.**
  - All 8 D4 maps and the stabilisers check out.
  - The canonical exits {0,1,2,6,7,12} match the server's.
  - The first tokens of the roots match the canonical direction mask.
- **-3 and -4 cannot occur in the campaign configuration.** The small table caps at 50% load, below the probe limit; `g_heap_cap` is unset; `--shortcut-state-cap` is not used.
- **Disabled prunes.** The mandatory-hole prune and HOLEBOUND are off in campaign builds.
- **Packing capacity.** 128-bit packing is safe on 5x5 (at most 20 blocks, 108 bits). 5x5 with at most 4 holes keeps the same 64-bit capacity (nb <= 11).
- **Solver optimisations.**
  - The reference-table prune and its XOR translation are sound, including with several holes.
  - Multi-start dominance and its fallback are sound.
  - Eviction is deterministic: evicting duplicate jobs matched exactly.
- **solver6**, the site's independent verifier, is sound under the worker's rules; no path leads it to report a longer optimum.
- **Server.**
  - There is no server-only path to a false "complete", and the first report wins.
  - The hot queries are indexed.
  - A restart mid-campaign is safe, and WAL with synchronous=NORMAL stays consistent.
- **Public pages.** There is no XSS and no static-path traversal, and every renderer's mask table matches the worker's.
- **Client internals.** Pipe handling, outbox atomicity, drop handling and Python 3.9 compatibility are sound.
- **Ruled out:**
  - overflow of the level-code and board buffers;
  - the MAX_BLOCKS=32 cap on 6x6;
  - server or client rejecting 6-column codes.
